# The LuxSync ROI Guide for Seniors, Caregivers, and Aging in Place

## Where Luxury Lives Intelligently

**For older adults, families, caregivers, and households planning for greater ease of use at home**

The best aging-in-place technology should respect independence, consent, privacy, dignity, and personal preference. Residential ROI may include easier daily tasks, less non-clinical assistance for routine environmental controls, measured energy savings, and earlier awareness of certain property conditions. It must never be framed as a replacement for care or emergency support.

## The Residential Senior ROI Case

| Value area | What to measure | Relevant LuxSync capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday ease | Tasks simplified for lighting, climate, shades, and selected devices | Accessible Living, voice/manual controls, routines |
| Nighttime movement | Ease of finding a softly lit path and number of manual lighting steps | Night Path, Goodnight |
| Comfort | Environmental adjustment requests and usability | Climate Intelligence, Gentle Morning, simple scenes |
| Family/caregiver coordination | Non-clinical check-in trips or calls related to home status | Property Pulse, consent-based notifications |
| Property awareness | Leak and temperature exceptions, response time | Water Watch, Property Pulse |
| Energy use | Lighting/climate operation when rooms or the home are unoccupied | Energy Intelligence, Away |

## Build the Baseline Together

With the resident's involvement and consent, document:

- daily home-control tasks that are difficult, tiring, or easy to forget;
- preferred physical, app, or voice controls;
- nighttime lighting needs and manual steps;
- non-clinical assistance related to lighting, climate, shades, entry, or property checks;
- energy use and typical occupancy patterns;
- current technology, Wi-Fi, SmartThings, hearing/vision/mobility considerations, and support comfort;
- who may access controls or receive notifications;
- what must continue to work during an internet or power interruption.

## A Respectful Pilot

Start with one or two tasks the resident chooses. Keep a simple manual option and test it in real conditions.

Potential first steps:

- bedside or hallway Night Path lighting;
- simplified Goodnight lighting and climate;
- easy-to-use lighting or shade controls;
- Water Watch notifications with a clearly agreed response plan;
- consent-based Property Pulse status for a second home or family-supported household.

Measure adoption, confidence, false alerts, support requests, and whether the resident actually prefers the new experience.
