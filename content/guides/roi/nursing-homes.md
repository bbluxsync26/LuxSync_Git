# The LuxSync ROI Guide for Nursing Homes

## Where Luxury Lives Intelligently

**For nursing-home owners, administrators, facilities leaders, operations teams, and care-technology stakeholders**

In a nursing-home environment, technology must support dignity, comfort, staff workflow, and responsible property operations. The ROI case should be grounded in measured operational improvement—not medical promises or assumptions that automation can replace people, clinical systems, or required safety infrastructure.

## The Nursing-Home ROI Case

| Value area | What to measure | Relevant LuxSync capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Staff workflow | Time spent on non-clinical environmental adjustments and repeatable room tasks | Accessible Living, lighting, climate, routines |
| Resident comfort | Environmental requests, room-comfort feedback, response time | Climate Intelligence, Gentle Morning, Goodnight, accessible controls |
| Nighttime environment | Lighting-related requests and pathway-lighting usability | Night Path, low-level lighting routines |
| Room readiness | Time and steps required to prepare rooms after a transition | Room-state routines, lighting, climate, Property Pulse |
| Energy operations | Lighting and climate use in rooms and common areas | Energy Intelligence, Climate Intelligence, Away states for appropriate spaces |
| Water/property awareness | Leak detection and facilities response | Water Watch, compatible sensors and shut-off capabilities |
| Facilities oversight | Manual checks, site walks, and exception response | Property Pulse, environmental and device-status visibility |

## Build the Baseline

Before a pilot, document:

- room count, common areas, floors, and operating zones;
- utility use by available meter or zone;
- non-clinical environmental requests and response times;
- staff time spent on repeatable room/environment tasks;
- room turnover or readiness time;
- water events and facilities response history;
- resident, family, staff, privacy, accessibility, consent, and regulatory requirements;
- existing nurse-call, fire/life-safety, security, building-management, and clinical systems that must remain independent unless an approved integration is specifically validated.

## Pilot With Governance First

Select a limited non-clinical use case and involve administration, facilities, IT/security, accessibility, privacy, care leadership, residents or representatives, and legal/compliance reviewers as appropriate. Define what the system may control, who receives alerts, what happens during connectivity loss, and how consent and access are handled.

Good pilot candidates may include:

- common-area lighting schedules;
- resident-controlled room lighting or climate where appropriate;
- Night Path lighting that does not interfere with care protocols;
- leak awareness in a limited zone;
- non-clinical room-readiness routines.

## Care and Safety Boundary

LuxSync solutions are not medical devices, nurse-call systems, fall-detection guarantees, clinical monitoring, emergency response, professional security monitoring, or substitutes for required staffing and life-safety systems. No automation should delay or replace clinical judgment, resident support, emergency procedures, or mandated equipment.
